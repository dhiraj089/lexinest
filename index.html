🪺 LexiNest

Smart NLP Text Analyzer — tokenize, tag, extract, visualise & disambiguate text

Input text (used by every module)

1 · Text Processing

Why: Raw text is messy. Tokenization splits it into units, filtration removes noise, and script validation confirms the writing system (vital for multilingual apps, spam & language filters).
How: Regex splits text into words/sentences; filters drop punctuation & digits; Unicode script properties count characters per script.

2 · Stop Words, Stemming & Lemmatization

Why: Search engines and classifiers work better after removing filler words and reducing words to a base form (“running”, “ran” → “run”).
How: A stop-word list is filtered out; the stemmer chops suffixes by rules (fast, may give non-words like “studi”); the lemmatizer uses irregular-form lookup + rules to return real dictionary words.

3 · Bag of Words & N-Grams

Why: Models need numbers. BoW counts words for classification/spam detection; N-grams keep word order for autocomplete, plagiarism and language models.
How: BoW = frequency of each unique word. N-gram = sliding window of N consecutive tokens.

4 · Part-of-Speech Tagging

Why: Knowing noun/verb/adjective helps grammar checkers, question answering and information extraction.
How: Penn-Treebank style tags are assigned using a word lexicon (the, is, and…) plus suffix and capitalization rules (-ing → VBG, -ly → RB).

5 · Named Entity Recognition

Why: Pulls out people, places, organizations, dates and money — used in news analysis, resume parsing and chatbots.
How: Capitalized-word chunks are classified using gazetteers (known places/orgs) and title cues (Dr., Mr.); regex patterns catch dates, money, percentages and emails.

6 · Exploratory Data Analysis (Word Cloud)

Why: A quick visual of what a document is about — used in survey, review and social-media analysis.
How: Stop words are removed, words counted, and font size is scaled by frequency.

7 · WordNet (Synonyms, Antonyms) & Brown Corpus

Why: Lexical databases power search-query expansion, thesaurus tools and semantic similarity. A corpus gives real-usage statistics.
How: A mini WordNet-style dictionary returns synonym sets (synsets) and antonyms; a mini Brown-style corpus (news, fiction, science) gives word frequency & concordance lines.

8 · Word Sense Disambiguation (Lesk)

Why: “bank” can mean money or river. WSD picks the right meaning for translation, search and voice assistants.
How: Simplified Lesk counts overlapping words between the sentence context and each sense's definition; the sense with the highest overlap wins.
