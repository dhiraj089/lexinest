Module 1

1 · Text Processing & Normalization

Fundamental tokenization, noise filtration, and Unicode script verification.

👤 Created by Dhiraj Jadhav
💡 Concept Deep Dive: What, Why & How
🎯 Why It Matters
Raw human language contains messy punctuation, whitespace inconsistencies, and formatting. Without tokenization, machines cannot separate words, numbers, or sentences for downstream processing.
⚙️ Mechanism
Tokenization: Uses regex lookaheads to identify word boundaries, punctuation, and sentence stops.
Filtration: Strips non-alphanumeric noise and standardizes letter casing.
Script Validation: Inspects Unicode script blocks (Latin, Devanagari, Arabic, etc.).
🚀 Real-World Application
Multilingual chatbots, input sanitization pipelines, spam classifiers, search indexing engines, and LLM BPE tokenizers.

2 · Stop Words, Stemming & Lemmatization

Reducing inflectional forms to root bases to condense vocabulary dimensionality.

👤 Created by Dhiraj Jadhav
💡 Concept Deep Dive: What, Why & How
🎯 Why It Matters
Words like "running", "runs", and "ran" represent the same semantic action. Combining them reduces vocabulary size and ensures search queries retrieve related variants.
⚙️ Stemming vs Lemmatization
Stemming: Uses heuristic suffix removal (e.g., chops -ing, -ed, -es). Very fast, but can produce non-dictionary roots (e.g. "studi").
Lemmatization: Performs morphological lookup to yield proper dictionary headwords (e.g., "ran" → "run", "better" → "good").
🚀 Real-World Application
Google Search indexing, document clustering, sentiment classification, legal document discovery, and keyword analysis.

3 · Bag of Words (BoW) & N-Gram Modeling

Feature representation transforming human sentences into mathematical frequency matrices.

👤 Created by Dhiraj Jadhav
💡 Concept Deep Dive: What, Why & How
🎯 Why It Matters
Machine learning models cannot read raw strings; they require numerical vectors. BoW represents text as token counts. N-Grams preserve local word ordering and phrase structure.
⚙️ Mechanism
BoW: Counts occurrences of each unique word irrespective of grammar.
N-Gram: Uses a sliding window of size $N$ across contiguous tokens (Bigram = 2 words, Trigram = 3 words).
🚀 Real-World Application
Spam filtering (Naive Bayes), predictive text autocomplete, plagiarism checking, and TF-IDF document retrieval.

4 · Part-of-Speech (POS) Tagging

Syntactic disambiguation assigning lexical categories based on context and morphology.

👤 Created by Dhiraj Jadhav
💡 Concept Deep Dive: What, Why & How
🎯 Why It Matters
Words can function as different parts of speech based on context (e.g., "book a flight" vs. "read a book"). POS tagging enables machines to understand sentence syntax.
⚙️ Mechanism
Assigns Penn Treebank tags using a core dictionary, morphological affixes (-ly $\to$ Adverb, -ing $\to$ Gerund), auxiliary verb mapping, and capitalization cues for proper nouns.
🚀 Real-World Application
Grammar checkers (Grammarly), speech synthesis (TTS pitch inflection), machine translation, and dependency parsing.

👤 Created by Dhiraj Jadhav

8 · Word Sense Disambiguation (Lesk Algorithm)

Resolving polysemy and finding the exact contextual meaning of ambiguous words.

👤 Created by Dhiraj Jadhav
💡 Concept Deep Dive: What, Why & How
🎯 Why It Matters
Many words have multiple meanings (polysemy). For instance, "bank" can mean a financial repository or the sloping side of a river. Machines need disambiguation to prevent humorous or erroneous interpretations.
⚙️ The Lesk Algorithm
Michael Lesk's algorithm calculates the word-overlap score between the target word's sentence context and candidate dictionary gloss definitions. The sense with the highest overlap is selected as the winning meaning.
🚀 Real-World Application
Machine Translation (picking correct translation for "bank" or "crane"), question answering, and conversational AI voice assistants.
